How Pangolin Money works

A plain-language tour of the design for our household finance app. It covers where the data lives, who can see what, how money gets counted, and how we get it all back if the server dies.

One box, one database

Everything runs as one program on our own Debian VM, behind the proxy we already use. It keeps a single SQLite file, and nothing else ever writes to it. The phone and browser app is just a view onto that program.

Whatever makes a change (the app, a background job or an admin command), it goes through the same use cases. Each one also writes an audit entry.
  • One path for every change. Importing a statement, editing a split, a job saving an LLM suggestion and a password reset all follow the same route. That route checks permissions and records who did what.
  • The maths is kept separate. Budget pace, capital gains and goal allocation are plain calculations with no database or network in them. They can be tested against hand-worked figures.
  • Slow things happen first. Talking to the LLM or fetching prices happens before any data is written, so a slow network never holds the database.

Who sees what

Each of us can mark an account private. Only its owner ever sees it, and to the other person it simply doesn't exist. Inside a shared account, either of us can hide a transaction's name for up to 12 months (a birthday present, say). The amount still counts in every total.   • Household totals are personal. Your net worth includes your private accounts, so the two of us can see different household numbers. That's intended.
  • Shared figures never include private money. A split in a private account can't be marked "shared". Shared budgets, the shared savings pool and the "who contributed what" split are therefore the same for both of us, and leak nothing.
  • Personal plans stay personal. Your own budgets, goals, pay details and tax pack are yours alone. Anything worked out from a private account is scoped to its owner.
  • Hidden really means hidden. A hidden name is removed before searching, sorting, grouping, rule matching or exporting. It isn't just blanked on screen, so search results can't give it away.
  • Payees and rules follow the same rule. A payee or rule created from a private transaction stays yours until you use it on a public account.

Getting data in

We don't hand bank logins to anyone. We export files from NetBank, ubank and the others, and Pangolin imports them. Re-importing the same file, or one that overlaps an earlier import, changes nothing.

  1. Read the file. OFX, CSV, QIF or a PDF statement. PDFs are read by the local LLM, then plain code checks that the opening balance plus every line equals the closing balance, to the cent.
  2. Spot duplicates. The bank's own transaction ID when there is one, plus a fingerprint of date, amount and description. If the same account, date and amount turn up from two formats, we get asked rather than guessed.
  3. Categorise. Our rules first, then the payee's usual category, then an LLM suggestion. Our own edits always win, and a rule never overwrites something one of us chose.
  4. Match transfers. Money moving between our own accounts is linked, so it doesn't count as spending.
  5. Check the balance. Each statement balance must agree with the one before it plus everything in between. Any gap goes to the review inbox.

Everything that needs a human lands in one review inbox: odd duplicates, low-confidence guesses, a missed bill, a savings shortfall, a failed backup. Items clear themselves once the underlying problem is fixed.

How money is counted

Money is stored as whole cents, never as floating-point numbers. Every total is worked out fresh from the transactions when you look at it, so there are no running totals to drift. A few rules make sure every screen agrees:

  • Splitting cents always adds up. When a figure is divided (goal shares, a 60/40 contribution split, a partly deductible expense), the parts always sum exactly to the whole.
  • Pay periods follow real paydays. Each of us picks either calendar fortnights or "whenever the pay actually lands". Budgets and goals in the same pool always agree on what "this fortnight" means. Financial years run July to June: FY2025 is 1 Jul 2024 to 30 Jun 2025.
  • One balance per account. Cash accounts start from the latest statement balance. Shares are units times price, and super is units times that day's unit price. Every report uses the same figure.

Savings and goals

Goals are virtual: there's no separate bank account per goal. At the end of each pay period, new savings in a pool are split across goals by the active stage's percentages. What's left stays in a buffer.

  • Pools follow ownership. A savings account with two owners feeds the shared pool, and a single-owner account feeds that person's pool.
  • Goals don't shrink when we overspend. A bad fortnight draws down the buffer only. If the buffer runs out, the goals page shows the shortfall in red.
  • A goal only goes down when we say so. Only a withdrawal from its savings account that we link to the goal reduces it. Spending on the Japan trip card doesn't touch the goal until the money is actually moved.
  • Closed periods stay closed. If an old transaction turns up later, the difference goes into this period's buffer as a logged adjustment. Past allocations never change.

If things go wrong

  • Lost phone or passkey. Use one of your ten recovery codes, or have the other partner issue a 24-hour re-enrolment link. The partner issuing that link could in theory use it themselves. We accepted that trade-off, and it's written down.
  • Server dies. At install you get a recovery bundle: the app key, login secret and backup password. Keep it offline, somewhere safe. CI proves on every release that a restore onto a clean machine works from that bundle alone, receipts and authenticator codes included.
  • Bad upgrade. The upgrade takes a snapshot first. If the new version fails its health check, it rolls back to the old version and the snapshot automatically.
  • What the design can't protect against. Whoever has admin access to the VM can read everything. Privacy between the two of us holds inside the app, not against the server's administrator.

Solution design · C4 views

Pangolin Money v1

A self-hosted, two-person household finance app for everyday banking, investments, super and an Australian tax pack. It runs as one Node process over one SQLite file on our own VM.

Status final, spine AD-1 to AD-27 Updated 27 Sep 2026 Governing spine ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md, AD-1 to AD-27 Feature contract specs/spec-pangolin-money/SPEC.md

Scope

This document is the readable companion to the architecture spine. It shows the system at three C4 levels and walks through the flows where the decisions interact. The spine is binding: if this document and the spine disagree, the spine wins. For what each feature does, the spec and its companions are the contract.

Paradigm: hexagonal (ports and adapters) with a functional core. Pure calculations live in domain. Use cases in app are the only write path. Database, file, price and LLM integrations are adapters, and apps/server wires it all together.

Period close for goals

  • A local-lane job runs per pool and period_start, idempotent on that pair (AD-8, AD-11).
  • It resolves the pool's PayCalendar and computes new savings in the pool's savings accounts (poolOf), excluding interest and linked withdrawals (AD-14, AD-24, AD-26).
  • It splits positive savings with allocate() by the active stage's shares, freezes the period's boundaries and records any stage change (AD-13).
  • It checks that goals plus buffer equal the pool's balance (balanceAsOf). A shortfall or mismatch raises a review item, scoped to the pool's person for a personal pool (AD-17, AD-19, AD-22).
  • Later backdated changes land as an audited adjustment in the current buffer (AD-11).

Restore

  • Stop the stack, take the exclusive lock on the data directory, restore into a fresh directory and verify it: PRAGMA integrity_check, then the manifest's counts, sums and balances.
  • Swap it in. Cancel every pending job with external effects and re-seed the recurring schedules before the server accepts work (AD-16).

Data architecture

Ownership

  • Each table has one owning module; the others read it or call that module's use cases (AD-10).
  • Classifying fields on a split change only through setSplitField, with precedence user > rule > payee > activity > llm.
  • Split IDs are stable across edits.

Privacy scope

  • Anything tied to an account takes that account's scope (AD-3).
  • Rows belonging to a person are visible only to that person. A derived row takes the most restrictive scope of its inputs (AD-22).
  • Classification rows keep the scope they were created in until the owner uses them on a public account (AD-18).

Derived vs stored

  • Balances, budgets, reports, forecasts and tax figures are computed when read.
  • The stored exceptions form a closed list: allocations, lots (rebuilt from events), recurring series, suggestions, review items, FTS indexes and closed-period boundaries (AD-11).

Money and time

  • Integer cents, signed from the account's view (AD-12).
  • Rounding happens only in toCents (half-even) and allocate (largest remainder) (AD-13).
  • Dates come from an injected Clock, periods are half-open, and FYs are labelled by the year they end (AD-14).

The full table catalogue is in the spec's data-model.md. Tables the spine adds: review_item, pay_anchor, goal_link, stage_event, forecast_assumption, fy_config, distribution_component, and scope columns (person_id, and account_id on audit_log).

Risks and follow-ups

Accepted residual risks. The VM administrator can read everything. The partner who issues a partner-assisted reset link could redeem it themselves. A "Transfer from <owner>" label can reveal by elimination that a private account exists.

Watch list

  • M1 import trust is the highest risk. The dedupe and snapshot-chain rules exist for it; give epic 3 disproportionate time.
  • Node 26 becomes LTS on 28 Oct 2026, before M0 ships.
  • Drizzle 1.0: move from 0.45 once 1.0 final ships.
  • The CSP depends on ECharts' rich-text tooltips and nonce'd style elements. The e2e CSP test guards against regressions.
